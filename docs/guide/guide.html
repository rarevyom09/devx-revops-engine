Chapter 1

Before you start

What the platform does, in one paragraph

A sales rep closes a deal and describes it in plain words, often messily ("₹18L build, then ₹75k a month support, Google Cloud co-sell"). That one sentence has to become a clean deal record, then a booking, then invoices, then cash collected, and finally a margin once delivery hours are logged. Today that chain is stitched together by hand in a CRM, spreadsheets and Slack, and money leaks at every hand-off. This platform lets the AI do the manual reading and drafting (splitting the deal, asking the rep what is unclear, drafting the delivery brief, billing milestones and follow-up emails), lets plain rules check every rupee and date, and keeps humans as the only ones who approve anything involving money.

The five stages you will see everywhere Deal (the rep's text, checked) → Booking (approved deal records) → Invoice (billing milestones raised) → Realization (cash collected before the deadline, or variable pay is clawed back) → Margin (price minus delivery cost).

There are no personal logins in this demo: one shared passcode, and dropdowns ("I am", "Viewing as") to choose who you are acting as.

The AI budget, and why some buttons say "uses 1 AI call"

The platform uses Anthropic's Claude (Haiku 4.5) for three jobs: reading a raw deal description, drafting an onboarding brief, and drafting a follow-up or "next step" message. Each run is one AI call. To keep costs fixed there is a hard cap of 100 calls for the whole project, shared by everyone. At the time of the screenshots 57 had been used and 43 were left. Automatic AI steps stop on their own when only the AI reserve (default 5 calls) is left, so the manual buttons always keep some budget.

3.9 Clawback Rules-based

For: making sure each invoice is paid in time. The rule: every invoice must be paid by the end of the quarter after it was raised, or the owner's variable pay on it (an invented 5% of the invoice) is clawed back in proportion to the unpaid share.

3.10 Margin Rules-based

For: comparing each deal's quoted price with its delivery cost from logged India (offshore, ₹2,000/h) and Singapore (onsite, ₹6,500/h) hours.

3.11 Honesty and the AI accuracy eval Data

For: knowing what is real, what is simulated, which assumptions still need confirming, and how accurate the deal-reading AI has measured. Read it before relying on any number.

AI accuracy (eval)

The Deal Integrity AI was run, through the same production pipeline (AI proposal, then code checks), on deal texts whose correct answers were written down in advance. The number that matters for money is the silent error rate: wrong proposals that look clean, so a human could approve them without being warned.

Chapter 4

Ingesting data

Three ways in: paste a deal as text, upload a CSV file, or type one row in a quick form. Plus the demo data buttons.

4a. Paste deal text

  1. Click Ingest in the sidebar. The Paste deal text tab is open by default.
  2. Paste or type the deal exactly as the rep wrote it into Deal text. Messy is fine.
  3. Choose the Consulting owner from the dropdown (for example "Daniel Tan (SG)"). The AI's questions go to this person's Rep inbox; if you leave it unassigned, only ops sees them.
  4. Click Add to pending queue.
Careful: this uses an AI callWith "Analyse new deals" on, Add to pending queue triggers the AI straight away (1 call). Up to 5 deals per upload are analysed automatically; the rest wait for a manual Analyse.
What you should seeA grey message: "Deal added. If auto-analyse is on, Claude is reading it now; you'll get a notification when it's ready for review." Within about 15 to 30 seconds the 🔔 count goes up with "<Client> analysed: …". If the AI has questions, the rep also gets "Question for rep" and the deal appears in their Rep inbox under Needs your answers. Ops sees it in Deal Integrity as ANALYSED and on the Deal Pipeline board under Needs review.

4b. CSV upload

  1. On Ingest, click the CSV upload tab.
  2. Pick the Target table (see the list below). The column guide underneath changes to match.
  3. Click Download template to get a CSV with the right headers and one example row. Fill it in with a spreadsheet app and save as CSV.
  4. Click Choose CSV… and select your file.
  5. Read the preview: the first 10 rows, each with a ✓ or a red error message. Nothing has been saved yet (this check is a dry run).
  6. If every row is valid, click Commit N row(s). If any row is invalid the button stays disabled: fix the file and choose it again.
Careful: Commit saves for realCommit writes every row in one go, or none at all if any row fails. A raw_deals CSV also triggers auto-analysis (1 AI call per deal, up to 5 per upload).

Chapter 5

Rep inbox: answering the AI's questions

For sales reps. The AI plays back what it understood from your deal and asks only what it could not work out. Your answers go straight into the deal, the AI re-checks it, and ops approves.

Step 1: choose who you are

  1. Click Rep inbox in the sidebar.
  2. In I am, pick your name (for example "Ananya Iyer (IN)"). There are no logins in the demo; the choice is remembered in this browser only.

Step 2: read the three groups

If the AI noticed that your own label disagrees with the text (for example you typed "one-time" but described monthly billing), an amber "About your label: …" note explains the correction. If the AI forgot to explain it, the code adds the note itself, starting with "(Added by checks)".

Step 3: answer and send (when you have questions)

No rep had open questions when these screenshots were taken, and creating one would have meant running the AI, so this step is described in words. A deal under Needs your answers shows the same read-back card as Figure 5.2, plus an amber heading "N questions for you" and one answer box per question:

  • Dates (for example "Is the first billing date 1 Feb 2027?") get a date picker.
  • Partner registration gets "Yes, registered / No, not yet"; deal type gets "One-time / Recurring".
  • Amounts, months and MDF get a number box; anything else gets a text box.
  • If your text mentions a foreign-currency amount (S$, SGD, USD, $), it is never converted. You are asked: "The deal mentions S$… What is the agreed INR amount? (No currency conversion is assumed.)"
  1. Fill in the answers you know (you can send some and leave others for later).
  2. Click Send N answers.
Careful: Send saves for realYour answers are saved on the deal's audit trail with your name, and ops gets a "Rep answered" notification.
Careful: sending triggers an AI re-checkWith "Analyse new deals" on and the budget above the reserve, the deal is re-analysed with your answers automatically: 1 AI call. The message says "Sent. The agent is re-checking the deal with your answers; ops is notified." Otherwise it says the answers went to ops without a re-check.
What you should seeThe card moves to Waiting for ops approval within about 10 to 30 seconds (the page refreshes itself every 10 seconds). Ops sees "Re-analysed with <your name>'s answers: high confidence" in the bell. This is exactly what happened to Kaveri: the AI asked "Has Google Cloud approved the deal registration?", Ananya answered "no", and the re-analysis came back at high confidence.

Chapter 6

Reviewing a deal in Deal Integrity

This is where the ops lead checks the AI's proposal and approves it. Nothing becomes a deal without this step. The screenshots show the real Kaveri Agritech phase 2 deal, analysed, answered by the rep and waiting for approval.

Step 1: open the deal

  1. Click the 🔔 notification (for example "Re-analysed with Ananya Iyer's answers") and then Open →, or go to Deal Integrity and click the deal in the queue (status ANALYSED).
  2. Read the Raw deal text at the top so you know what the rep actually said.

Step 2: read the AI analysis panel

Other notes you may see in this panel: an amber Rep label conflict (the rep's own label disagrees with the text; if the AI did not explain it, the note starts with "(Added by checks)"), and flags for a foreign-currency amount (the record's amount is left empty and a question asks for the agreed INR value; nothing is ever converted).

Step 3: questions for the rep

When the AI had to assume something, a Questions for the rep card appears under the analysis. Kaveri's current analysis has none (Ananya's answer was used in the re-run), so the two pictures below are an example from earlier demo data (the Zephyr deal, since removed). The card works the same way today, with one addition: an answer sent from the Rep inbox shows a green "answered by <rep>" tag.

Step 4: check and edit the proposed records

Use + Add record to add a record by hand, or Remove to drop one. Errors (missing amount, recurring without months, bad date) show in red under the record and block approval.

Step 5: set the practice split

Type a percentage for each practice involved (AI, Web, Cloud, Data). If the total is not 100 a red message appears and Approve stays disabled. You may leave all boxes blank to approve without a split, but it will then show as an attribution leak.

Step 6: approve (or reject)

  1. Type your name in Approved by.
  2. If a checkbox appears ("I reviewed the failed checks above" or "I accept N open questions unanswered"), read the flags and tick it. It only appears when something is unresolved; for Kaveri (high confidence, no flags, no open questions) it does not.
  3. Click Approve N records. To discard the deal instead, click Reject at the top.
Careful: Approve commits money recordsApproval is the only way into the deals table. It is visible to everyone at once, and the rep's inbox moves the deal to Approved.
Careful: approving triggers AIWith "Draft brief on approval" on, each approved record gets an onboarding brief drafted automatically: 1 AI call per record (2 for a blended deal like Kaveri).
What you should seeThe panel changes to "Approved deal records" with your name. Shortly afterwards the bell shows "Brief drafted: …" for each record, and the deals appear in Onboarding and on the board under Booked.
Chapter 7

Onboarding: brief, milestones, invoices

Approved deal in → AI drafts the brief and billing milestones → code checks the money schedule → a human approves → invoices are scheduled.

  1. Click Onboarding in the sidebar and pick a deal on the left. Its pill says no brief, draft or approved.
  2. If the brief was auto-drafted (pill "draft"), it opens in the editor. If there is no brief, click Draft with AI (1 AI call) or Write manually (no AI, starts from a template).
  3. Review the Open questions to resolve (amber box) and confirm them with the client or rep before kickoff.
  4. Edit the scope summary, deliverables, success criteria and risks (one item per line).
  5. Edit the Billing milestones: name, %, due date. The INR amounts are computed by code; the AI never types money.
  6. Make the validator green: percentages must total 100, dates must be valid, in order, not before today and not after the project end, names must be unique.
  7. Type your name in Approved by and click Approve brief & schedule invoices. Save draft keeps your edits without approving.
Recurring dealsFor a retainer, the milestone table is a monthly schedule generated by code from the term and first billing date. You cannot edit the percentages; edit the deal itself if the schedule is wrong.
Careful: approval is finalThe brief becomes read-only and each milestone becomes a scheduled invoice (mock rows, not sent to any accounting system). Variable pay at stake is set to 5% of each invoice.
What you should see after approvingThe deal pill turns "approved", invoices appear under the brief, and on Deal Pipeline the card moves from Booked to Onboarded (or Invoicing once the first invoice date has passed).

Chapter 9

Checking the Manual Loop

Each Try case has a side-by-side walkthrough comparing how the same deal moves today (CRM, spreadsheets, Slack) with how it moves in this tool. It is static: it reads no data and uses no AI.

  1. On Try cases, click See Manual Loop on any case card. It works whether or not the case is loaded.
  2. Click Play walkthrough to reveal one step on each side every ~1.2 seconds, or use Next → and ← Back to step at your own pace. Play turns into Pause and Resume.
  3. Watch the clock line ("Manual: 16 days elapsed · AI-native: 4 h") and the red Leak notes (manual) vs green Caught notes (AI-native).
  4. Click Skip to results to jump to the comparison tiles at the end.
  5. Close with ✕ or the Esc key.
Read the results honestlyThe timings are hand-written from stated assumptions (month-end forecast review, 1–2 day Slack loops, finance clawback run ~30 days after quarter close). They are illustrative, not measured from Devx's real process.

Margin what-if

  1. Open Margin and find the deal's row.
  2. Type extra hours in +IN or +SG under "What-if: extra hours" (Figure 3.14).
  3. Read the new Margin % ("was …" shows the real value) and Status.
  4. Click Clear what-ifs when done. What-ifs live only in your browser and are never saved.

Currency switch

On Settings, click SGD or USD under Display currency. Every ₹ amount on screen is re-shown at the fixed demo rate (1 SGD = ₹65, 1 USD = ₹84, as of 2 Oct 2026). Inputs and stored data stay in INR, and the choice applies only to your browser. Click INR to switch back.

Display currency is not deal currencyThe switch only changes how numbers are shown. If a deal itself is priced in S$ or USD, the AI does not convert it: it leaves the amount empty and asks the rep for the agreed INR value (see Chapter 5).

Chapter 12

Troubleshooting and FAQ

I see "AI unavailable: manual entry". What happened?

The AI could not run. Common reasons: the 100-call budget is used up ("budget_exhausted"), the API key is missing ("not_configured"), the AI declined or returned something that failed the format check ("refusal", "invalid_output", "truncated"), or a network or API error. Nothing is guessed. What to do: fill in the records (Deal Integrity) or the brief (Onboarding, pre-filled with a 40/40/20 template or a monthly schedule) yourself. The same checks and approval apply.

The AI calls are running out.

When the calls left reach the AI reserve (default 5, set on Settings), all automatic AI steps stop on their own and you get a notification such as "Auto-analyse paused: AI reserve reached"; manual AI buttons still work until 0. At 0 every AI button falls back to manual entry. Save calls by: not re-analysing after answering questions (answers already fill the fields), not restarting AI Try cases, and using Write manually in Onboarding. Only the demo owner should change the switches.

Where did the automation switches, currency and AI meter go?

They moved from the sidebar to the ⚙︎ Settings page (Figure 2.3). The sidebar keeps a small badge next to Settings with the calls left.

I'm a rep. Where do I answer questions?

In the Rep inbox: pick your name in "I am" and look under Needs your answers. The 🔔 "Question for rep" notification links straight there. Ops can also answer on your behalf in Deal Integrity.

My deal says "The integrity agent is reading this deal…" for a long time.

It has not been analysed. Either automation is off or paused at the reserve, the upload had more than 5 deals, or the deal came from the demo seed (which never runs AI). Ask ops to click Analyse with AI in Deal Integrity (1 AI call).

What is a "follow-up draft", and was it sent?

No, nothing is ever sent. When a serious leak appears (clawback risk, missed deadline, margin below floor, renewal due), the AI drafts the message a person would send and shows it in Diagnose. Read it, check the ₹-figure warning, Copy message into your own email, then click Mark sent (or Dismiss).

I'm locked out / the passcode doesn't work.

Type devx-revops-2026 exactly: lower case, two hyphens, no spaces. If the page keeps returning to the passcode box, your browser may be blocking cookies for the site; allow them or try a normal (not private) window. Lock app signs you out on purpose.

Why are the numbers in S$ or US$?

Someone (maybe you) switched Display currency to SGD or USD on the Settings page in this browser. It is display only, at fixed demo rates; switch back to INR. All inputs and stored data are always INR.

My deal is priced in S$ or USD. Why is the amount empty?

On purpose. Amounts in another currency are never converted by the AI or the code. The record is created with an empty amount and the rep is asked: "What is the agreed INR amount? (No currency conversion is assumed.)" Answer with the INR figure agreed with finance.

Why is a deal in this column on Deal Pipeline?

Columns are worked out from the data, never set by hand: Intake = raw text not analysed; Needs review = analysed, not approved; Booked = approved but no approved brief and no invoices; Onboarded = billing plan set but nothing raised yet; Invoicing = some invoices raised, cash outstanding; Collected / Live = everything raised so far is paid. To move a card, do the next thing on its "Next:" line.

What does "added by checks" mean?

The AI assumed something (for example a date it was not told, or an unknown partner registration) without asking. Code spotted the assumption and added the question itself, so assumptions cannot reach the books unnoticed. Similarly, a label-conflict note that starts with "(Added by checks)" means the rep labelled the deal one-time but the terms include monthly billing, and the AI did not explain the change, so the code added the explanation.

What does "capped by checks" mean?

The AI rated its own confidence higher than the code checks allow (for example because a date was assumed). The code lowered it. The badge always shows the lower, safer value.

Why is Approve greyed out?

One of: no name in Approved by; a record has a red error; the practice split does not total 100%; or an acknowledgement tick is needed ("I reviewed the failed checks above" or "I accept N open questions unanswered"). In Onboarding: validator flags are red, or no name.

My CSV says "missing required column" or every row is red.

Download the template for the same target table and copy your data into it. Check dates are YYYY-MM-DD, locations are IN or SG, and deal names match an approved deal exactly (the Quick form's dropdown shows the exact names).

Approving a brief didn't create invoices.

The deal already had invoices, so the platform skipped creating new ones to avoid double billing (an amber note says so). Reconcile the existing invoices by hand.

How accurate is the AI?

See Honesty → AI accuracy (eval) (Figure 3.16): on a small held-out set of 6 invented deals, 0 silent errors, 5/6 fully correct, and the one wrong case was caught. The sets are small and invented, so treat this as evidence the checks work, not as a guarantee.

How do I start fresh?

  1. Go to Ingest.
  2. Click Reset and confirm "Delete ALL rows in every table?". This affects everyone.
  3. Click Load demo data. No AI is used.

To restart only one scenario, use Restart case on Try cases instead: it reloads just that case's clients (and, for AI cases, re-runs the analysis).

Chapter 13

Glossary

Blended deal
One description that mixes one-time work and recurring work (for example a build plus monthly support). The platform splits it into separate records so recurring revenue is not booked as one-time.
One-time vs recurring
One-time: a fixed fee for a project, stored as a total. Recurring: a monthly amount for a number of months (a retainer), stored as monthly amount × term. ARR (annual recurring revenue) = monthly × 12.
Close date rule
For a one-time record the close date is the project end (go-live); for a recurring record it is the first billing date. If the text only gives a month, the date is assumed and turned into a question for the rep.
Booking vs invoicing vs realization
Booking: an approved deal record (contracted value). Invoicing: billing milestones raised as invoices. Realization: cash actually collected against those invoices, before the deadline.
Clawback
If an invoice is not fully paid by the end of the quarter after it was raised, the owner loses the matching share of the variable pay tied to it (variable pay at stake = an invented 5% of the invoice). Clawback = at stake × (1 − paid by deadline ÷ invoice amount). "Exposure" is the clawback that would happen if nothing more is paid; "at risk" means the deadline is within 60 days.
Double bubble
Credit for invoiced revenue goes 100% to the consulting owner and to the practice teams by their split, so total credit is about twice the revenue by design. A deal with no practice split is an attribution leak.
Practice split
How the practice credit is shared across AI, Web, Cloud and Data; must total 100%.
MDF
Market Development Funds: money a partner (for example AWS) contributes towards a deal. It is partner funding, not revenue, so it is kept out of deal amounts and tracked as something to claim.
Deal registration
Registering a co-sell deal with the partner so the partner attribution and benefits apply. Unregistered partner deals raise a warning.
Co-sell
Selling a deal together with a partner such as AWS, Microsoft or Google Cloud.
Blended rate
The average hourly delivery cost across India and Singapore hours: (IN hours × ₹2,000 + SG hours × ₹6,500) ÷ total hours.
Margin floor / target
Margin % = (price − delivery cost) ÷ price. Below the 30% floor is a critical warning; the target (plan, default 40%) is what the deal should make. Erosion = target − actual.
Rep inbox
The page where a sales rep sees what the AI understood from their deals and answers its questions. Answers are written into the deal, saved with the rep's name, and trigger an automatic re-check.
Read-back
The plain-language summary in the Rep inbox of what the AI understood ("One-time project … for ₹18,00,000, ending 31 Mar 2027"), with "(assumed)" where it guessed.
Follow-up draft
A message (payment chase, change request or renewal note) the AI drafts automatically when a serious leak appears. Shown in Diagnose with a ₹-figure check; never sent by the platform.
₹-figure check
Code that compares every ₹ amount in an AI-written message with the deal's real data and lists any that don't match.
Added by checks
A question or note the code added because the AI assumed something without saying so (an assumed date, unknown registration, an unexplained label override).
Foreign-currency flag
When a deal mentions S$, SGD, USD or $, the amount is never converted; the record's amount is left empty and the rep is asked for the agreed INR amount.
Validator / checks
Plain code that re-checks every amount, date, type, name and partner field the AI proposes, and every billing milestone. Failed checks force low confidence or block approval.
Silent error
An AI proposal that is wrong but looks clean (no flag, not low confidence, no question), so a human could approve it. The eval's headline metric.
AI reserve
The number of AI calls kept back for manual use; automation pauses when only this many are left (default 5, set on Settings).
As-of date
A "time machine" date on Dashboard and Clawback. Shows what deadlines and exposure would look like on that date; payments after it are ignored. Nothing is saved.
AI call
One request to the AI. The project has a hard cap of 100, shared by everyone.
